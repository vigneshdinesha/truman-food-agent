# Synthetic & Added Colors

## Red 40 (Allura Red AC, E129)
Red 40 is a synthetic azo dye, the most widely used color additive in the US food
supply, common in candy, sodas, cereals, and snacks. It is approved by the US FDA
and by EFSA in Europe, which set an Acceptable Daily Intake (ADI) of 7 mg/kg body
weight per day. It is not a whole-food ingredient and carries no nutritional value.
The main evidence-based concern: some controlled studies (notably the UK "Southampton
study") associated mixtures of synthetic dyes plus the preservative sodium benzoate
with increased hyperactivity in some children, which led the EU to require a warning
label on foods containing certain dyes. For most people at normal intakes it is
considered safe by regulators; sensitive individuals and parents of children with
attention issues may prefer to limit it. Source: US FDA color additive status; EFSA
re-evaluation of Allura Red AC (E129).

## Yellow 5 (Tartrazine, E102)
Tartrazine is a synthetic azo dye used in candies, drinks, chips, and packaged foods.
FDA-approved; EFSA ADI 7.5 mg/kg/day. It is one of the dyes implicated in the
Southampton hyperactivity findings and can trigger allergic-type reactions
(hives, itching) in a small subset of sensitive people, particularly some with
aspirin sensitivity. FDA requires tartrazine to be declared by name on labels
because of this sensitivity. Generally recognized as safe at normal intakes for the
general population. Source: US FDA; EFSA re-evaluation of tartrazine.

## Yellow 6 (Sunset Yellow FCF, E110)
Synthetic azo dye used in snacks, sauces, and beverages. FDA-approved; EFSA ADI
4 mg/kg/day (lowered after re-evaluation). Same general profile as other azo dyes:
implicated in the Southampton hyperactivity study; rare sensitivity reactions.
Safe for the general population at regulated levels. Source: EFSA re-evaluation of
Sunset Yellow FCF.

## Blue 1 (Brilliant Blue FCF, E133)
Synthetic triarylmethane dye used in drinks, candy, and dairy. FDA-approved; EFSA
ADI 6 mg/kg/day. Poorly absorbed by the gut; considered low-concern by regulators.
No strong evidence of harm at normal intakes. Source: EFSA re-evaluation of
Brilliant Blue FCF.

## Titanium Dioxide (E171)
A white pigment/opacifier used to brighten candy, gum, and some processed foods.
Still permitted in the US (FDA), but in 2022 the EU BANNED it as a food additive
after EFSA concluded it "could no longer be considered safe" because a genotoxicity
concern (potential DNA damage from nano-sized particles) could not be ruled out.
This is a case where US and EU regulators diverge — worth flagging to a user as a
"concerning / regulators disagree" ingredient. Source: EFSA 2021 opinion on E171;
EU Regulation 2022/63.

## Caramel Color (E150a–d)
The most-used color additive by weight worldwide, giving colas and sauces their brown
hue. Four classes exist; class III and IV (made with ammonia) can contain the
byproduct 4-methylimidazole (4-MEI), which California lists under Prop 65 at high
exposure. At typical dietary intakes regulators consider it safe; heavy cola drinkers
have the highest exposure. Source: FDA; California OEHHA Prop 65 listing for 4-MEI.
