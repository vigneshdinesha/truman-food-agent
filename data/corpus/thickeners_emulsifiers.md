# Thickeners, Stabilizers & Emulsifiers

## Mono- and Diglycerides (E471)
Emulsifiers made from fats, used to blend oil and water in bread, ice cream, and
spreads. FDA GRAS. Mostly benign, but two notes: they can be a **hidden source of trans
fat** (their manufacture can produce small amounts, and they're exempt from trans-fat
labeling), and some can derive from animal or plant fat (a concern for vegan/religious
diets). Verdict: generally low concern, worth-knowing for trans-fat-avoiders. Source:
FDA GRAS.

## Polysorbate 80 (E433)
A synthetic emulsifier in ice cream, sauces, and supplements. FDA-permitted. Animal and
lab studies have suggested that emulsifiers like polysorbate 80 and carboxymethyl-
cellulose may disrupt the gut microbiome and promote intestinal inflammation; human
evidence is still emerging. Verdict: worth-knowing, especially for those with gut
sensitivity. Source: FDA; emerging research on dietary emulsifiers (e.g., Chassaing et
al.).

## Carboxymethylcellulose / Cellulose Gum (E466)
A plant-derived thickener widely used in low-fat and gluten-free foods. FDA GRAS and
generally considered safe, but it's the other emulsifier (with polysorbate 80) flagged
in gut-microbiome research. Verdict: low-to-worth-knowing. Source: FDA; dietary-
emulsifier research.

## Guar Gum (E412) & Locust Bean Gum (E410)
Plant-seed thickeners used in dairy, sauces, and gluten-free baking. Both FDA GRAS,
well tolerated, and essentially soluble fiber; large amounts can cause gas/bloating.
Verdict: low concern. Source: FDA GRAS; EFSA re-evaluations.

## Pectin (E440)
A fiber-based gelling agent from fruit, used in jams and yogurts. Natural, FDA GRAS,
and considered a clean, benign additive. Source: FDA GRAS.

## Maltodextrin
A carbohydrate made from corn, rice, or wheat starch, used as a filler, thickener, or
bulking agent. It is **highly processed and has a very high glycemic index** (can spike
blood sugar more than table sugar), which matters for people managing blood glucose,
though it's not "toxic." Verdict: worth-knowing for blood-sugar-conscious eaters.
Source: FDA; glycemic-index data.

## Modified Food Starch
Starch chemically or physically altered to improve texture/stability. The name sounds
alarming but it is FDA-permitted and generally low-concern; "modified" does not mean
genetically modified. Verdict: low concern. Source: FDA 21 CFR 172.892.

## Cellulose / Cellulose Gel (microcrystalline cellulose, E460)
Purified plant fiber used as an anti-caking agent and bulking fiber (e.g., in shredded
cheese to prevent clumping). Inert and FDA GRAS; sometimes criticized as "wood pulp"
filler, but it is safe dietary fiber. Verdict: clean/low concern. Source: FDA GRAS.
