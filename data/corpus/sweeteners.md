# Sweeteners (Added Sugars & Artificial/Non-Nutritive)

## High-Fructose Corn Syrup (HFCS)
A liquid sweetener made from corn starch, roughly 55% fructose / 45% glucose in the
soda form — metabolically similar to table sugar (sucrose). Ubiquitous in sodas,
sweetened drinks, and processed foods. The concern is not exotic chemistry but
QUANTITY: like all added sugars, high intake is linked to weight gain, type 2
diabetes, and fatty liver disease. Major health bodies (WHO, American Heart
Association) recommend limiting all added sugars to well under 10% of calories.
HFCS is best treated as "added sugar" — the issue is dose, not that it's a scary
additive. Source: WHO guideline on sugars intake; AHA added-sugar recommendations.

## Aspartame (E951)
A low-calorie artificial sweetener (~200x sweeter than sugar) used in diet sodas and
"sugar-free" products; molecular formula C14H18N2O5. FDA-approved with an ADI of
50 mg/kg/day; EFSA ADI 40 mg/kg/day. In 2023 the WHO's IARC classified aspartame as
"possibly carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2B) — but the WHO/FAO JECFA committee
simultaneously reaffirmed the existing ADI, meaning intakes within normal limits are
considered safe. People with the genetic condition PKU must avoid it (it contains
phenylalanine). Balanced verdict: extensively studied, safe at normal intake for most
people; heavy diet-soda drinkers may prefer to moderate. Source: FDA; EFSA 2013
re-evaluation; WHO/IARC & JECFA 2023 statements.

## Sucralose (Splenda, E955)
A non-caloric artificial sweetener (~600x sweeter than sugar) made by chlorinating
sucrose. FDA-approved; EFSA ADI 15 mg/kg/day. Long considered inert, but more recent
research raised questions about effects on gut microbiota and, in some studies, that
heating sucralose (baking) may generate undesirable compounds. Regulators still
consider it safe at normal intakes. Reasonable "worth knowing" rating. Source: FDA;
EFSA scientific opinions on sucralose.

## Acesulfame Potassium (Ace-K, E950)
A non-caloric artificial sweetener often blended with sucralose or aspartame. FDA-
approved; EFSA ADI 9 mg/kg/day. Generally regarded as safe by regulators; some
animal studies prompted calls for further research, but no established human harm at
normal intakes. Source: FDA; EFSA re-evaluation of acesulfame K.

## Saccharin (E954)
One of the oldest artificial sweeteners. Once carried a warning label after 1970s rat
studies linked it to bladder tumors, but that mechanism was later found NOT to apply
to humans, and it was removed from the US list of carcinogens in 2000. Now considered
safe at normal intakes. A good example of a scare that the science later overturned.
Source: US National Toxicology Program delisting (2000); FDA.

## Stevia / Steviol Glycosides (E960)
A non-caloric sweetener extracted from the stevia plant — one of the few "natural"
high-intensity sweeteners. FDA GRAS for purified steviol glycosides; EFSA ADI
4 mg/kg/day. Generally well tolerated and considered a lower-concern choice. Note
that whole-leaf/crude stevia is NOT FDA-approved — only purified extracts are.
Source: FDA GRAS notices; EFSA opinion on steviol glycosides.
