# Banned, Restricted & Controversial Additives

## Red 3 (Erythrosine, FD&C Red No. 3, E127)
A synthetic cherry-red dye. In January 2025 the US FDA **revoked authorization**
for Red 3 in food and ingested drugs, because it caused cancer in male rats and the
Delaney Clause prohibits any additive shown to cause cancer in animals — even though
the mechanism is considered not relevant to humans at typical exposure. It was already
banned from cosmetics decades earlier. If you see Red 3 on an older product, it is on
its way out. Verdict: concerning / being phased out. Source: FDA 2025 revocation of
FD&C Red No. 3.

## Partially Hydrogenated Oils (artificial trans fats)
The primary source of artificial trans fat, once common in margarine, fried foods,
and baked goods. Trans fats raise LDL ("bad") cholesterol and lower HDL, strongly
raising heart-disease risk. The FDA determined PHOs are **no longer Generally
Recognized As Safe** and effectively **banned** them from the US food supply
(compliance completed ~2020); the WHO has pushed for global elimination. Verdict:
concerning — but largely removed from US products already. Source: FDA final
determination on PHOs; WHO REPLACE trans-fat initiative.

## Brominated Vegetable Oil (BVO, E443)
An emulsifier once used to keep citrus flavoring suspended in some sodas. In 2024 the
FDA **revoked its authorization** for use in food after studies raised concerns about
bromine accumulating in body tissue. Already banned in the EU and elsewhere. Verdict:
concerning / banned. Source: FDA 2024 revocation of BVO.

## Potassium Bromate (E924)
A flour "improver" that strengthens dough. Classified by WHO/IARC as **possibly
carcinogenic (Group 2B)**; **banned** in the EU, UK, Canada, and many other countries.
Still permitted federally in the US, but California's 2023 Food Safety Act bans it
(effective 2027). A clear "regulators disagree / avoid" ingredient — most large US
bakers have voluntarily stopped using it. Source: IARC Monograph; California Food
Safety Act (2023).

## Azodicarbonamide (ADA)
A dough conditioner and flour-bleaching agent — nicknamed the "yoga mat chemical"
because it is also used to make foamed plastics. Permitted in the US up to 45 ppm;
**banned in the EU and Australia**. Concern centers on breakdown products
(semicarbazide, urethane) formed during baking. Verdict: worth-knowing to concerning;
US/EU regulators disagree. Source: FDA 21 CFR 172.806; EFSA opinion.

## Propylparaben (E216)
A preservative. **Banned as a food additive in the EU** after its acceptable intake
was withdrawn over endocrine-disruption concerns; still used in some US products and
also restricted under California's 2023 Food Safety Act. Verdict: worth-knowing;
regulators diverge. Source: EFSA; California Food Safety Act (2023).

## Sulfites (Sulfur Dioxide, Sodium Bisulfite/Metabisulfite, E220–228)
Preservatives/antioxidants in dried fruit, wine, and some processed foods. Safe for
most people, but a recognized trigger for **asthma attacks and allergic-type reactions
in sensitive individuals**, so the FDA requires sulfites above 10 ppm to be declared on
the label and bans them on fresh produce. Verdict: clean for most, concerning for the
sulfite-sensitive. Source: FDA sulfite labeling regulation.
