# Preservatives & Antioxidants

## Sodium Benzoate (E211)
A common preservative that inhibits mold and bacteria in acidic foods (sodas, salad
dressings, pickles). FDA GRAS; EFSA ADI 5 mg/kg/day. Two evidence-based notes: (1) it
was part of the dye+benzoate mixtures linked to child hyperactivity in the Southampton
study; (2) in the presence of vitamin C (ascorbic acid) and heat/light, it can form
trace amounts of benzene, a carcinogen — an issue flagged in some sodas, since
addressed by reformulation. Safe at regulated levels for most people. Source: FDA;
EFSA re-evaluation of benzoic acid/benzoates.

## Potassium Sorbate (E202)
A widely used, well-tolerated preservative against mold and yeast in cheese, baked
goods, and drinks. FDA GRAS; considered one of the lower-concern preservatives, with
low toxicity and no significant health flags at normal intakes. Source: FDA; EFSA
re-evaluation of sorbic acid/sorbates.

## TBHQ (tert-Butylhydroquinone, E319)
A synthetic antioxidant that keeps fats and oils from going rancid, common in fried
and packaged snack foods. FDA-permitted up to 0.02% of the oil/fat content; EFSA ADI
0.7 mg/kg/day. Some animal studies at high doses raised concerns, and it appears on
many "additives to limit" lists, but human intake at label levels is far below limits
regulators consider unsafe. Reasonable "worth knowing," especially for heavy consumers
of fried/packaged snacks. Source: FDA 21 CFR 172.185; EFSA re-evaluation of TBHQ.

## BHA and BHT (E320, E321)
Butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA) and butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) are synthetic
antioxidant preservatives in cereals, chips, and fats. Both are FDA-permitted. BHA is
listed by the US National Toxicology Program as "reasonably anticipated to be a human
carcinogen" based on animal (forestomach) tumors — though the relevance to humans is
debated because humans lack that organ. This US/animal-data flag makes BHA a common
"concerning / worth-avoiding" ingredient on health-focused lists. Source: US NTP
Report on Carcinogens (BHA); FDA; EFSA re-evaluations.

## Sodium Nitrite / Sodium Nitrate (E250 / E251)
Curing agents that preserve processed meats (bacon, hot dogs, deli meat), prevent
botulism, and fix the pink color. This is one of the more evidence-backed concerns:
during high-heat cooking, nitrites can form nitrosamines, which are carcinogenic. The
WHO/IARC classifies PROCESSED MEAT as a Group 1 carcinogen (sufficient evidence for
colorectal cancer), and nitrite curing is part of why. A legitimate "concerning"
flag, especially for frequent processed-meat eaters. Source: WHO/IARC Monograph 114
(processed meat); EFSA opinion on nitrites.
